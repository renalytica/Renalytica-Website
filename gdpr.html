ARTICLE 03 // FRAMEWORK

3. What is GDPR & International Alignment

The General Data Protection Regulation is a landmark European privacy law enacted to give individuals greater control over their personal data. It establishes enforceable requirements for organizations regarding data minimization, purpose limitation, transparency, robust security, and prompt notification in the event of an information security breach.

Under the GDPR, individuals have legal rights to know what data is held about them, how it is being used, to whom it is disclosed, and to demand its correction or complete erasure where applicable. Renalytica enforces these high-watermark European standards uniformly across its global operations, ensuring that clients outside the European Economic Area (EEA) benefit from identical enterprise safeguards.

ARTICLE 04 // METHODOLOGY

4. How Renalytica Implements GDPR by Design

Privacy and data protection are engineered into Renalytica’s technology architecture from the initial software development lifecycle (SDLC) through production deployment. Our operational implementation encompasses four foundational pillars:

Pillars of Renalytica Privacy by Design:
  • Strict Tenant & Role Isolation: Microservice separation ensures customer accounts, billing details, and purchased licenses cannot be cross-referenced or accessed by unauthorized actors.
  • Cryptographic Security: All client data in transit is protected using bank-grade TLS 1.3 transport encryption, and all stationary database volumes are encrypted using AES-256 cipher suites.
  • Dynamic Deliverable Watermarking: Downloaded PDF publications and Excel models are personalized with cryptographically signed corporate serial identifiers to prevent unauthorized digital leakage while ensuring provenance.
  • No Data Monetization Policy: Renalytica does not monetize personal data, maintain ad-tracking pixels, or sell organizational contact details to data brokers or third-party advertisers.

ARTICLE 06 // LAWFUL GROUNDS

6. Purpose and Lawful Bases for Processing

Under GDPR Article 6, every processing operation conducted by Renalytica is justified by at least one recognized lawful basis:

  • Performance of a Contract (Article 6(1)(b)): Processing necessary to fulfill our obligations under purchase orders, enterprise subscription agreements, and single-user publication licenses.
  • Compliance with Legal Obligations (Article 6(1)(c)): Processing required to maintain audited commercial financial records, satisfy statutory tax obligations, and comply with binding regulatory directives.
  • Legitimate Interests (Article 6(1)(f)): Processing essential for maintaining cyber defense postures, preventing fraudulent multi-user account abuse, and safeguarding our intellectual property.
  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)): Explicit, affirmative opt-in consent for optional research wire newsletters, webinar invitations, and non-essential analytical cookies (which may be withdrawn at any time).

ARTICLE 08 // USER RIGHTS

8. Your Statutory Data Rights Under GDPR

As a data subject under GDPR, you possess extensive statutory rights regarding your personal data. Renalytica upholds and facilitates the exercise of each right without undue delay:

🔍
Right of Access (Art. 15)
Request complete confirmation of whether your data is being processed, along with a full digital copy of all personal records held by Renalytica.
✏️
Right to Rectification (Art. 16)
Correct or update any inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated personal or organizational records through self-service settings or direct request.
🗑️
Right to Erasure (Art. 17)
The "Right to be Forgotten"—request complete and permanent deletion of your account and identity records, subject to statutory commercial tax retention mandates.
⏸️
Right to Restrict Processing (Art. 18)
Temporarily freeze or restrict the active processing of your data while an accuracy dispute or legal claim is being investigated.
📦
Right to Data Portability (Art. 20)
Receive all personal information you provided to us in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format (JSON or CSV) for transfer to another provider.
🛑
Right to Object (Art. 21)
Object at any time to the processing of your personal data based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing communications.

ARTICLE 10 // INTERNATIONAL TRANSFERS

10. Cross-Border Data Transfers & Supervisory Recourse

Given Renalytica’s multinational client base spanning African economic corridors, Europe, the United Kingdom, and the Americas, personal data may occasionally be transferred across international borders. Whenever personal data originating in the European Economic Area (EEA) or United Kingdom is transferred internationally, Renalytica ensures appropriate legal safeguards are implemented in compliance with GDPR Chapter V:

  • Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs): Execution of the European Commission's approved standard contractual clauses with all non-EEA partners and sub-processors.
  • Data Transfer Impact Assessments (DTIAs): Regular quantitative risk evaluations assessing local surveillance legislation and technical encryption measures.
  • Supervisory Authority Recourse: European residents have the legal right to lodge a complaint with their domestic data protection supervisory authority (such as the CNIL in France, BfDI in Germany, or the Data Protection Commission in Ireland). UK residents may lodge complaints with the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO).
