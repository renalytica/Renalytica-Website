### Step 1: Gathering Real Facts (Primary Surveys + Secondary Resources)
* **Simple Summary:** Where our data comes from and how we capture it.
* **What We Do:**
  * **Primary Field Surveys & Ground Nodes:**
    * *On-the-Ground Merchant Surveys:* We deploy trained enumerators to collect fresh weekly pricing, turnover volumes, and inventory numbers directly from wholesale markets, distributor depots, and farming cooperatives across 180+ commercial hubs.
    * *Direct Industry Interviews:* Structured qualitative and quantitative inquiries with factory managers, freight haulers, trade association executives, and port clearing agents.
    * *Official Shipping & Port Manifests:* Real cargo movements tracked through customs declarations, bills of lading, and port authority data to measure physical volumes entering and leaving each market.
    * *Satellite Remote Sensing:* Satellite vegetation indexing (NDVI), soil moisture telemetry, and rainfall imagery to verify crop yields and farm health independently from the sky.
  * **Authoritative Secondary Resources:**
    * *Institutional Economic Registers:* Direct data ingestion from Central Banks, National Bureaus of Statistics, and Finance Ministries across target countries.
    * *Multilateral Datasets:* Structured records from the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, African Development Bank (AfDB), and the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO).
    * *Audited Corporate & Regulatory Filings:* Annual financial statements, stock exchange disclosures, bond prospectuses, and official government gazettes to verify corporate market share and capital investments.
    * *Specialized Industry Repositories:* Historic commodity spot prices, trade federation publications, and verified academic research papers.
  * **The Triangulation Advantage:**
    * Secondary resources give us broad historical context and macroeconomic structure; primary surveys give us real-time ground truth. When the two are triangulated, anomalies in official secondary statistics are instantly exposed, and self-reporting bias in field surveys is corrected.

### Step 2: Cleaning and Cross-Checking the Numbers
* **Simple Summary:** Removing errors, weird spikes, and currency distortions.
* **What We Do:**
  * **Adjusting for Currency Swings:** Because local currencies can fluctuate rapidly, we calculate values in both local currency and US Dollars using volume-weighted parallel and official rates, ensuring trends reflect real purchasing power rather than currency devaluation.
  * **Harmonizing Varied Source Formats:** Aligning disparate datasets—such as converting customs metric tonnage into standard retail units and standardizing national fiscal calendars into unified time-series models.
  * **Removing Seasonal Spikes:** Food and commodity prices naturally drop during harvest and spike during lean months. We adjust for regular seasonal patterns so you see the underlying multi-year trend.
  * **Reconciling Source Discrepancies:** If a secondary government bulletin reports a 40% jump in domestic grain production, but our primary field surveys show market price spikes and port logs show surging grain imports, our research desk investigates until the empirical discrepancy is fully reconciled.

### Step 3: Testing Realistic Scenarios (Not Just Best-Case Guesses)
* **Simple Summary:** Testing what happens when things go wrong.
* **What We Do:**
  * **No Single-Point Guesses:** Markets are unpredictable. Instead of just giving one optimistic forecast, our economic models test hundreds of combinations of fuel prices, rainfall levels, and exchange rates.
  * **Three Clear Paths:** For all key forecasts, we provide:
    * *The Base Case:* What is most likely to happen based on current market trends.
    * *The Conservative (Worst-Case) Scenario:* How your business is protected if prices or exchange rates turn against you.
    * *The Growth (Best-Case) Scenario:* The upside potential if expansion plans succeed.
  * **Clear Margins of Error:** We show you the realistic range for every 5-to-10-year projection so your risk team knows what to expect.

### Step 4: Senior Analyst Double-Check & Plain-English Translation
* **Simple Summary:** Real human expertise, zero confusing jargon.
* **What We Do:**
  * **Two Economists Sign Off:** Every chart, table, and projection must be independently reviewed and approved by two senior economists before it goes into a published report.
  * **Translating to Plain English:** We remove unnecessary academic formulas and replace them with clear, simple takeaways that you can explain to your board in two minutes.
  * **Ready-to-Use Files:** We provide everything in clean, easy-to-use formats: high-resolution PDFs, unlocked Excel spreadsheets with transparent data citations, and presentation-ready slide decks.

## 5. Sector-Specific Verification Protocols

### Agriculture & Agribusiness
* **Primary Inputs:** Field enumerator surveys across 180+ rural farming cooperatives, wholesale grain depot audits, satellite biomass (NDVI) scans.
* **Secondary Resources:** Ministry of Agriculture seasonal crop bulletins, FAO GIEWS crop monitors, commodity board trade registers, import/export customs clearance forms.

### Macroeconomics & Foreign Exchange
* **Primary Inputs:** Direct FX bureau spot rate sampling, commercial bank liquidity desk inquiries, freight corridor transport tariff logs.
* **Secondary Resources:** Central Bank statistical bulletins, IMF Article IV consultation reports, sovereign debt debt-servicing schedules, National Bureau of Statistics consumer price index (CPI) series.

### Commerce, Retail & FMCG Logistics
* **Primary Inputs:** Retail shelf velocity spot checks across modern supermarkets and traditional open markets, distributor warehouse offload tracking.
* **Secondary Resources:** Audited corporate earnings filings from listed FMCG firms, port container throughput manifests, National Bureau of Statistics retail trade censuses.
