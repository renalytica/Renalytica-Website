## 1. The Problem: Information Asymmetry at the First Mile

  Sub-Saharan African agricultural production is overwhelmingly powered by smallholder farmers operating plots between 0.5 and 3.0 hectares. In Nigeria alone, smallholders cultivate more than 85% of total domestic food crops. Yet, these primary producers routinely capture less than 28% of the final urban retail value of their harvest.

  The principal driver of this severe economic value extraction is not agricultural incompetence; it is an extreme **first-mile information asymmetry**. When rural farmers harvest perishable tubers, vegetables, or cereal grains, they are geographically isolated from major urban consumption centers. Lacking access to real-time wholesale price benchmarks, smallholders are forced to sell their produce at rural assembly markets to traveling middlemen and informal commodity brokers who systematically underreport market clearing prices in Lagos, Kano, and Port Harcourt.

  Furthermore, because smallholder households often require immediate cash to pay school fees, healthcare expenses, or input debts, informal aggregators exploit this liquidity urgency. They purchase crops at severe distress discounts, transport the produce 400 miles along interstate corridors, and capture profit margins exceeding 60% on wholesale resale.

## 2. Core Program Pillars: How Project AgroTelemetry Works

  Unlike conventional corporate philanthropy that distributes perishable food aid or one-off equipment grants, Project AgroTelemetry is designed as a self-sustaining technological empowerment framework built directly on top of Renalytica’s commercial data engine:

### Pillar 1: Zero-Rated USSD and SMS Price Feeds

  Through strategic partnerships with major regional telecommunications carriers (MTN Nigeria and Airtel Africa), Renalytica has deployed a toll-free USSD service (`*384*22#`) and automated bi-weekly SMS broadcasts. Smallholder farmers with basic feature phones (2G devices) can query real-time wholesale spot prices at major regional trading hubs—including Dawanau (Kano), Bodija (Ibadan), Mile 12 (Lagos), and Zaki Biam (Benue)—completely free of mobile airtime charges.

### Pillar 2: Community Solar-Powered Grain Moisture Probes

  To protect smallholders from quality discount penalties imposed by commercial aggregators, Renalytica has installed 120 community-managed, solar-powered digital moisture testing kiosks at rural cooperative collection points. Prior to selling grain, farmers can test their maize and soybean moisture levels using lab-calibrated digital sensors, obtaining an automated printout certifying the moisture grade. This objective data prevents middlemen from arbitrarily docking 10% to 20% of bag weights under the pretext that grain is "too wet."

### Pillar 3: Cooperative Aggregation Collective Bargaining Desks

  Renalytica’s CSR field team trains cooperative leaders to pool individual farmgate harvests into 30-ton truckload lots. Armed with daily terminal market pricing data, cooperatives bypass predatory village brokers entirely and contract directly with institutional industrial buyers, commercial flour mills, and beverage manufacturers, securing institutional delivery prices that were previously inaccessible to smallholder families.
